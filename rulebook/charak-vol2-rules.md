# Rule book: Elements of Vedic Astrology, Volume 2 (K.S. Charak)

A structured index of the interpretive and technical rules in Volume 2 of K.S. Charak's *Elements of Vedic Astrology* (3rd edition, UMA Publications, 1998). It is a study aid for turning classical indications into checkable rules. It is not used by the website.

## How to use this file

- **Wording.** Each indication is a short summary in our own words, not a quotation. Read the book for the full text and its caveats.
- **References.** `Ch` is the chapter (Roman numeral) and the printed page where it starts. `b` is the block number in the private extraction (`charak-elements-vol2.md`, kept outside git). A block is usually one printed page.
- **IDs.** Stable keys for later use in code: `L5-H9` = lord of the 5th in the 9th house; `SU-H10` = Sun in the 10th house; `MO-M3` = Moon-based placement, 3rd from the Moon; `JU-Kark` = Jupiter in Karka; `ASP-MO-Mesh-JU` = Jupiter aspecting the Moon in Mesha.
- **The book's own cautions** (Ch XVII, "Points to remember"), which apply to every rule here:
  1. A planet that rules two houses gives results for both.
  2. If the two lordships point to contradictory results, those results cancel.
  3. If they point to different (not contradictory) results, both can occur.
  4. Strong planets give full results; weak planets give weak results.
  5. Results arrive in the dasha of the planets concerned.
  6. Classical results should be read in a modern context (for example, the 11th lord in the 12th can mean an elder sibling living abroad, or import and export).
- Results for planets in houses and signs are traditional indications. The author says repeatedly that they must not be applied literally, and that a chart is read by synthesis, never from one factor.
- Where the scan is unreadable, the rule is marked *[scan unreadable]*.

## Ch XVII. Placement of lords of houses (p. 229, b11 to b25)

**General principle.** A house lord placed in another house links the matters of both houses: the house it rules and the house it occupies (b11). Before applying a result, also weigh:
- the planet's natural character;
- its conjunctions and aspects;
- its condition in the divisional charts;
- the running dasha, which decides when the promise arrives (b11 to b12).

Each table lists the house the lord occupies, the summarised indications, and the book's qualifiers.

## Ch XVIII. Planets in houses (p. 245, b26 to b38)

**General principle.** The result comes from the planet's own nature meeting the significations of the house, regardless of what the planet rules. Apply with modern adjustment (b26).

**A general rule stated with the Sun in the 9th:** a significator placed in its own house of signification tends to damage that signification (*karako bhava nashaya*). For example, the Sun, karaka of the father, in the 9th house of the father (b27).

## Ch XIX. Planets in signs, and aspects on them (p. 260, b39 to b66)

**General principle.** A sign modifies the nature of the planet in it. The book stresses that these results must not be applied literally. Read them in today's terms: "favour from the ruler" can mean promotion in a government job, and "death of the spouse" can mean separation or divorce (b66).

**Grids.** For each planet, the first table gives its results by sign. The second table gives the effect of an aspect from each other planet, grouped by the sign the aspected planet occupies. The book itself asks whether aspects that are impossible in the rashi chart (Mercury or Venus aspecting the Sun) were meant for divisional charts (b39).

Sign names used: Mesha (Aries), Vrisha (Taurus), Mithuna (Gemini), Karka (Cancer), Simha (Leo), Kanya (Virgo), Tula (Libra), Vrischika (Scorpio), Dhanu (Sagittarius), Makara (Capricorn), Kumbha (Aquarius), Meena (Pisces).

## Ch XX. Nabhasa yogas (p. 291, b67 to b77)

**General principle.**
- Nabhasa yogas describe the overall pattern of a life. They do not depend on lordship, conjunction or aspect.
- They act throughout life, not only in particular dashas.
- Examine them before the detailed reading.
- Only the seven planets count; Rahu and Ketu are left out.
- There are 4 groups and 32 yogas (1,800 finer varieties) (b67).

**Precedence when yogas overlap (b75):**
1. An Aakriti yoga overrides a Sankhya yoga formed by the same placements.
2. An Aakriti yoga overrides an Aashraya yoga.
3. Kedaara, Shoola and Yuga (Sankhya) lapse if an Aashraya yoga is also present. Gola, however, overrides an Aashraya yoga.

**Dual signs (b69):** for these yogas, treat the first half of a dual sign (0° to 15°) as fixed and the second half (15° to 30°) as movable.

2. mutual aspect;

## Ch XXIII. Mathematical calculation of longevity (p. 338, b107 to b121)

**Book's caution (b111, b121).**
- Mathematical longevity formulas are often wrong in real charts.

### Three-pair method (b107 to b109)

**Step 1.** For each pair, look at the sign types of its two members:

| Pair | Members |
| --- | --- |
| I | Lagna lord and 8th lord (the signs they occupy) |
| II | Lagna and Moon |
| III | Lagna and Hora lagna |

**Step 2.** Read the span from the two sign types:

| Sign types of the pair | Span |
| --- | --- |
| Movable + movable | Full |
| Fixed + fixed | Short |
| Dual + dual | Medium |
| Movable + fixed | Medium |
| Movable + dual | Short |
| Fixed + dual | Full |

**Step 3: decision rule.**
- Two or three pairs that agree decide the span.
- If all three differ, take pair III.
- If all three differ and the Moon is in the 1st or 7th, take pair II.

**Hora lagna for this method (b107 to b108):**
- Take the time elapsed from sunrise to birth. Hours become signs (mod 12); minutes ÷ 2 become degrees.
- Even lagna: add this to the lagna cusp.
- Odd lagna: add it to the Sun's longitude.

**Mantreshwara's alternative (b109 to b110).** The same table, using these pairs:
1. Drekkana signs of the lagna and the Moon.
2. Navamsha signs of the lagna lord and the Moon-sign lord.
3. Dwadashamsha signs of the lagna lord and the 8th lord.

The book says this method needs research.

### Calculation methods (b111 to b120), in outline

- **Pindayu.** Each planet gives fixed years at deep exaltation and half that at debilitation, in proportion to its distance from debilitation. Four reductions (*haranas*) follow, for combustion, enemy sign, placement in houses 7 to 12, and a malefic in the lagna. Exceptions and fractions are given in the book.
- **Amshayu.** Each longitude in minutes ÷ 200 gives years (mod 12). Exaltation or retrogression trebles a planet's years; vargottama or own-sign placement doubles them; apply only the largest. The same reductions follow, except the one for a malefic in the lagna.
- **Nisargayu.** Fixed years per planet.
- **Choosing a method:** use Amshayu if the lagna is the strongest of lagna, Sun and Moon; Pindayu if the Sun is; Nisargayu if the Moon is.

The book's verdict: these methods are unreliable (b121).

- the lords of the 22nd drekkana and the 64th navamsha;
- the 8th lord's companions and its bitter enemy.

It also names dasha-order patterns to watch. These are study notes only.

## Ch XXIX. Gochara: transits (p. 414, b167 to b174)

**Principle.**
- The birth chart holds the promise, the dasha unfolds it, and transits time it.
- Transits never override the chart or the dasha (b167, b170).
- The classical texts count transits from the natal Moon. The author finds that counting from the lagna also works, and is more personal; apply both (b167).

### Favourable houses and vedha (obstruction) (b167 to b168)

Each favourable house is paired with a vedha house. A planet in the paired vedha house blocks the good result. Likewise, a planet in the favourable house blocks the bad result of a transit through the vedha house.

### From the examples (b171 to b174)

- Transits "switch on" houses. A transit of Jupiter or Saturn aspecting a natal house activates it.
- The Moon's transit pins the exact day.
- Jupiter or Rahu transiting the 11th from the Moon acts as protection.
- When fixing a wedding date, keep the 8th from both the lagna and the Moon free of affliction. The book's example: five planets in the 8th from the bride's Moon, and the wedding fell through (b174).

## Ch XXX. Ashtakavarga (p. 423, b175 to b185)

**Principle.**
- Ashtakavarga is mainly a transit tool. It always ranks below the birth chart and the dashas (b175, b184).
- Seven planets plus the lagna are the eight reference points; Rahu and Ketu are left out.
- Calculate from the sign (rashi) positions, not bhava positions (b184).
